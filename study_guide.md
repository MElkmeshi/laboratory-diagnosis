## Part 2: Term Definitions

*Know the definition, mechanism, and clinical relevance of each term.*

### Fat Saponification
A pathological process in which free fatty acids combine with calcium to form soap-like deposits in necrotic tissue, leading to inflammation, fat necrosis, adhesions, and **hypocalcemia** — especially in acute pancreatitis.

### Factor V Leiden
An inherited coagulation disorder caused by a mutation in Factor V that makes it resistant to inactivation by activated protein C, resulting in increased risk of **thrombosis and hypercoagulability**.

### Kernicterus
A severe neurological disorder caused by deposition of **unconjugated bilirubin** in the brain (especially the basal nuclei), resulting in neuronal damage, seizures, and possible coma or death in severe hyperbilirubinemia.

### Reverse T3 (rT3)
An **inactive** form of triiodothyronine produced by peripheral conversion of T4. Its concentration rises during **euthyroid sick syndrome** due to altered thyroid hormone metabolism during systemic illness (low T4, low/normal T3, normal TSH).

### Post-renal Azotemia
An increase in BUN and creatinine caused by **obstruction or rupture of the urinary tract** after urine formation, leading to reduced excretion of nitrogenous waste products.

### Inappropriate Absolute Polycythemia
A persistent increase in red blood cell mass caused by **excessive erythropoietin production** associated with renal tumors and EPO-secreting neoplasms.

### Anisocytosis
A variation in the **size of circulating RBCs**, commonly observed in regenerative anemias and other hematologic disorders. RDW-cv > 14.5% indicates anisocytosis.

### Obstructive Jaundice
Jaundice caused by **partial or complete blockage of bile flow** in the biliary system, leading to accumulation of conjugated bilirubin in blood and tissues.

### Photosensitization in Ruminants
A condition in which photodynamic substances (e.g., phylloerythrin) accumulate in the skin and react with sunlight, causing dermatitis, erythema, edema, necrosis, and sloughing of non-pigmented skin — commonly due to **liver dysfunction or toxic plant ingestion**.

### Myxedema Coma
A severe, life-threatening form of hypothyroidism characterized by extreme thyroid hormone deficiency leading to hypothermia, bradycardia, depression, hypoventilation, and **metabolic/neurologic failure**.

### Renal Tubular Acidosis Type IV
Caused by **hypoaldosteronism or aldosterone resistance**, resulting in impaired distal tubular secretion of H⁺ and K⁺ → **metabolic acidosis + hyperkalemia**.

### Agglutination
Irregular clumping of RBCs caused by **antibody-mediated binding** between erythrocytes — commonly associated with IMHA and incompatible blood transfusion reactions.

### Budd-Chiari Syndrome
A vascular disorder caused by **obstruction of hepatic venous outflow** → liver congestion, hepatomegaly, ascites, abdominal pain, and progressive liver dysfunction.

### Exocrine Pancreatic Insufficiency (EPI)
Inadequate production/secretion of pancreatic digestive enzymes (lipase, amylase, proteases) → **maldigestion, chronic diarrhea, weight loss, polyphagia, and malabsorption** despite normal/increased appetite. Common in dogs & cats.

### Pelger-Huët Anomaly
A hereditary disorder characterized by **hyposegmented neutrophil nuclei** with usually normal leukocytic function.

## Part 3: Matching Exercises

### 3A — Bleeding & Coagulation Disorders

| Disorder | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| Inappropriate Secondary Polycythemia | Too many RBCs due to high EPO — not caused by hypoxia; mainly acute red cell carcinoma |
| Gray Platelet Syndrome | Rare inherited bleeding disorder due to lack of alpha granules → macro-thrombocytopenia, bleeding tendencies, splenomegaly |
| Hermansky-Pudlak Syndrome | Rare genetic disorder with dual defect: albinism + lack of platelet granules |
| Factor V Leiden | Genetic mutation → thrombophilia (DVT, PE, shock) |
| Antiphospholipid Antibody Syndrome | Lupus anticoagulant paradox: prolonged clotting times *in vitro* but excessive bleeding *in vivo* |
| Hemophilia | Rare inherited or acquired bleeding disorder due to lack of a clotting factor |

### 3B — Liver Conditions

| Condition | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Steroid-induced hepatopathy (dogs) | Unique liver condition caused by excessive glucocorticoid exposure |
| Feline lipidosis (Fatty Liver Syndrome) | Caused by anorexia → rapid fat mobilization to liver for energy |
| Hepatic encephalopathy | Reversible neurological disorder caused by liver failure or shunting |
| Kernicterus (infants) | Permanent brain damage from excessive bilirubin during neonatal period |
| Rotor Syndrome | Rare, benign genetic disorder → chronic conjugated hyperbilirubinemia (non-itching jaundice, no liver damage) |
| Gilbert's Syndrome | Common, harmless condition where liver doesn't process bilirubin properly |
| Pyrrolizidine Alkaloids toxicity (horse) | Chronic, irreversible liver failure from toxic plants → weight loss, edema, photosensitization |
| Wilson's disease-like (sheep) | ATP7B mutation → copper accumulation due to inefficient biliary excretion |
| Hepatic carcinoma | Sudden increase of ALP + GGT |

### 3C — Renal Disorders

| Condition | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Renal tubular acidosis Type I (distal RTA) | Distal tubules fail to secrete H⁺ → metabolic acidosis + **hypokalemia** |
| Renal tubular acidosis Type II (proximal RTA) | Proximal tubules fail to reabsorb HCO₃⁻ → systemic metabolic acidosis |
| Post-renal azotemia | Elevated BUN/creatinine due to impaired outflow/obstruction |
| Fanconi Syndrome | Defect in PCT reabsorption: amino acids, glucose, phosphate, bicarbonate |

### 3D — Hematology

| Term | Description |
|------|-------------|
| Fanconi Anemia | Rare genetic disease — cells cannot repair DNA damage → aplastic anemia |
| Evans Syndrome | Rare autoimmune disorder — immune system destroys RBCs and platelets simultaneously (AIHA + ITP) |
| High RDW-cv (>14.5%) | Indicates anisocytosis |
| Mentzer Index | MCV (fL) ÷ RBC count (millions/µL) — differentiates IDA from beta-thalassemia |
| Pappenheimer Bodies | Abnormal iron-containing basophilic granules within RBCs (Wright-Giemsa stain) |

### 3E — Miscellaneous

| Condition | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| Intestinal bacterial overgrowth (IBO) | Consumes nutrients → Vitamin B12 deficiency, iron deficiency, fat malabsorption |
| EPI | Weight loss + polyphagia + steatorrhea |
| SIRS | Inflammation attacks blood vessel linings → MODS |
| Myxedema Coma | Medical emergency — extremely low thyroid hormone → total metabolic and neurologic breakdown |
| Euthyroid Sick Syndrome | Low total T4, but **normal TSH** |
